The research is not a credential. It is where the product starts.

Our team is world leading in computer vision, machine learning and deep learning, and combines academic researchers with people who have shipped commercial software. Between us we have written more than 250 scientific papers and several books.

That matters because the hard part is rarely the model. It is knowing what a camera can physically resolve, what a scan can be trusted to measure, and where the answer stops being reliable. We tell you that before you build on it.

Three industries where a millimetre changes the decision.

Each of these started as a research question and ended as software someone uses on a working day. The measurement, not the model, is the deliverable.

Healthcare

Millions of people suffer burn injuries every year. Treatment depends on two numbers: how much body surface is burned, and how deep. The hospitals that measure this properly use 3D laser scanners that cost between 20,000 and 100,000 euro, so most clinicians still estimate by eye.

Our scanning technology measures burned surface area objectively and in real time, at low cost. Patients can also scan at home before and during treatment, so specialists can review the 3D area remotely instead of asking them to travel.

Industrial inspection

Our software measures area, volume and colour on the production line and finds visual defects without removing parts or interrupting manufacturing. Once the 3D scan completes, it analyses the data and locates the deformity accurately.

Built with industrial partners including AkzoNobel, where a defect that is caught on the line is worth more than a report written the following week.

Retail and eyewear

Accurate sizing and body measurement from ordinary cameras, for manufacturing personalised products, reducing returns on online orders, and running virtual try on in real time.

For eyewear the geometry has to be right to the millimetre, since a frame that fits is a function of pupillary distance, bridge and temple length rather than a picture that looks convincing.
